    What if it runs out?

    Further costs in these 3 years are self-paid; a new allowance starts afterwards. Disability Certificate holders can also ask about disability subsidies (not for the same item)

    When does the 3-year period start?

    It starts from the care plan approval date — ask your case manager

    What if the device costs more than the government price cap?

    Each device has a price cap. You pay anything above it, but the excess isn’t deducted from your allowance

    Can I switch to smart assistive technology later?

    The choice is fixed for 3 years. If you used the NT$40,000 group before June 30, 2026, you can switch only after that period ends

    Smart assistive technology

    What it is, what it covers, how to rent and what it costs

    What it is Scope How to apply & use Costs How to choose FAQ

    In a nutshell

    Beyond the functions of ordinary devices, these sense, record and send alerts— e.g. alerting family when someone gets up, or reminding to reposition. These devices are mainly provided by rental; swap them as needs change and return them when done

    Sensing Detects bed exit, falls, posture or activity
    Recording Keeps usage and activity records
    Alerts Notifies family or caregivers when something is wrong
    Feedback & analysis Organizes data to help adjust care
    Scope: five categories Mobility, transfer, bathing & toileting, care beds, safety monitoring
    Mobility

    Power wheelchairs and mobility aids that track use and flag problems

    Transfer

    Help getting from bed to wheelchair or toilet

    Bathing & toileting

    Assist with bathing, hygiene or toileting care, with alerts

    Home care beds

    Care beds or mattresses with bed-exit and repositioning alerts

    Safety monitoring

    Detects falls, bed exit or unusual routines and alerts family

    The first 17 approved products cover mobility, bathing & toileting, care beds and safety monitoring, with more to come. Only products in the “Long-term Care Assistive Device Product Database” qualify
    How to apply & use 5 steps, from assessment to swapping or returning
    1. Confirm eligibility Eligible for LTC and assessed at LTC need level 2 or above (call 1966 for an assessment)
    2. Professional assessment A Class A assistive device assessor confirms suitability and issues a “Smart Assistive Technology Assessment Report”
    3. Choose Group 2 & get approval The LTC Management Center approves the care plan; the Group 2 allowance covers 3 years
    4. Delivered by a contracted supplier The supplier recommends, fits, delivers and explains the device — ask for a demonstration
    5. Follow-up, swap or return Regular check-ups and cleaning during rental; swap when needs change, return when done
    How costs work Group 1 NT$40,000, Group 2 NT$60,000 — choose one

    Group 1 · Conventional devices

    Every 3 years NT$40,000
    • Buy or rent
    • Widest range: wheelchairs, air mattresses, care beds, grab bars and more
    • Suits families with clear, long-term needs

    Group 2 · Smart assistive technology

    Every 3 years NT$60,000
    • Smart devices are mainly rented
    • Of the NT$60,000, at most NT$20,000 can go to home accessibility
    • Shower chairs, commodes, canes and walkers can still be bought
    Billing Billed by actual rental days, recorded on a “Rental Service Record” you sign
    Who sets the rent Each supplier sets and files its own rent, so prices vary
    Your share As for LTC devices: general 30%, low- and middle-to-low-income 0%, half-rate allowance recipients 10%
    No switching Choose one of the two groups; the group cannot be changed for 3 years; Group 1 approvals before June 30, 2026 must finish their 3-year period first
    Indigenous areas & outlying islands Rent may be increased by 20%; the extra 20% requires no co-payment and is not deducted from the allowance

    FAQ 8 most frequently asked questions
    Can any smart product on the market be subsidized?

    No. Products must pass MOHW review (functions, medical device licensing, inspection, telecom and app security) and be listed in the “Long-term Care Assistive Device Product Database”

    With Group 2, can I still buy conventional devices?

    Only basics: shower chairs, commodes, raised toilet seats, canes and walkers. Other conventional devices are Group 1

    Can we swap if the condition improves or worsens?

    Yes — swapping as needs change is the point of full rental. Ask your LTC care manager or supplier for reassessment

    What if the equipment breaks?

    Contact the supplier. Under the MOHW model contract, rental repairs are the supplier’s job — ask how to report faults when signing

    Will it collect my family’s data?

    They record and send usage data; app products must pass security testing. Before renting, ask where data is kept, who sees it, who gets alerts and what happens after return

    What does the home need?

    The MOHW says smart devices need stable internet and a caregiver who can use them. Ask the supplier to check your home first

    If Group 2 doesn’t suit us, can we switch back to Group 1?

    No. The group is fixed for 3 years, so discuss your needs with the assessor first

    Must smart devices always be rented?

    Mostly. Hygiene items for bathing & toileting that can’t be disinfected may be bought if under NT$5,000 (up to NT$5,000 per 3 years)

    Based on MOHW announcements; the LTC Management Center and suppliers have the final word. Check eligible products in the Long-term Care Assistive Device Product Database, or call 1966

    Home accessibility modification

    Where is home hard to move around or slippery? See what can change and the rough subsidy

    What it is Room-by-room check How to apply Find contractors Renting & apartments Safety tips

    In a nutshell

    So that your family member can move around, bathe and use the toilet at home, the government subsidizes small changes like grab bars, threshold leveling, anti-slip treatment and bathroom modification Every item must first be assessed at home by a professional; work can start only after approval

    LTC Group 1 NT$40,000 every 3 years, shared between devices and home modifications from this one allowance
    LTC Group 2 NT$60,000 every 3 years, of which home modification is capped at NT$20,000
    Disability subsidy Disability Certificate holders can apply under disability subsidies — not for the same item as LTC
    Minimum use period Most items can be reapplied for after 10 years; a few, like anti-slip, after 3

    Tap a room and tick the problems to see which subsidy items may apply. Amounts are general-household caps; actual amounts follow assessment and approval

    How to apply 5 steps — work starts only after approval
    1. Apply For LTC call 1966; Disability Certificate holders can also go to the district office or assistive technology center
    2. Home assessment An assessor visits, measures and writes an assessment report recommending what to change
    3. Wait for approval Receive the approval notice before starting work. Work done before approval may not be subsidized
    4. Use a contracted contractor LTC contractors usually charge only your share; disability claims vary by city/county
    5. Keep receipts Keep invoices and approvals; some areas need before-and-after photos. Ask before work starts which documents are needed

    Renting & apartments: FAQ Landlord consent, common areas, no elevator
    Can renters apply?

    You can ask, but modifying the property requires the landlord’s consent. Some areas need a landlord consent form and lease (New Taipei has a template). Ask your center or LTC care manager first

    What about social housing or special buildings?

    Social housing and heritage buildings need the managing authority’s consent first; Taichung won’t pay for work done without it

    Can apartment stairs and corridors be modified?

    Stairwells and corridors are shared property: you need the building committee’s consent and code compliance. Subsidies focus on your family member’s living space — check with the assessor

    What if we live upstairs without an elevator?

    LTC can cover stair-climber rental (per trip or monthly); Disability Certificate holders may be assessed for a stair climber, stair lift or platform lift. All need assessment of stair width and caregiver ability

    Can LTC and disability subsidies be used together?

    Different items can be applied for separately, but the same item cannot be subsidized twice. If eligible for both, ask an assistive technology center to plan the best combination

    What if it doesn’t fit after the work?

    Before work, have the assessor and contractor agree on positions and heights; test when finished. Report defects to the contractor and subsidy questions to your LTC care manager

    Safety tips 3 things to know before work
    Don’t DIY structural changes Leave walls, plumbing, wiring and bathroom structure to professionals — also needed for subsidies
    Grab bars must be secure Grab bars must hold full body weight, fixed to a load-bearing wall at the assessed height
    Shorter ramps aren’t better Too steep a ramp can tip or slide — ask the assessor about length and slope

    Based on LTC Benefit Regulations Schedule 4, the disability device subsidy schedule and MOHW announcements. Practices vary; confirm with 1966, your district office or an assistive technology center before applying. Full item list: “Home accessibility” category in All assistive devices

    Find assistive technology centers & suppliers

    Find assistive technology centers and LTC-contracted suppliers near you. Names and addresses stay in Chinese, as registered — handy for map apps or taxi drivers

    What is a “contracted supplier”? Tap to read

    Contracted suppliers have contracts with local government for subsidized devices. LTC purchases, rentals and home modifications must use them to get the subsidy

    • Convenient Bring your approval letter — you pay only your share and the supplier claims the rest
    • How to identify them Look for the “LTC Contracted” sign, or ask your LTC care manager for the list
    • Disability assistive devices Contracted suppliers can deduct the subsidy directly; or pay in full and claim yourself — varies by city/county
    • Ask before buying Either way, you must get approval before buying. A non-contracted shop may not qualify for the LTC subsidy — ask your LTC care manager or center first

    Source: SFAA Assistive Technology Resource Portal “National List of Assistive Technology Centers” (March 20, 2026 edition); Hsinchu County North Center from its website. Details may change; Please call before you go

    How to confirm an LTC-contracted supplier

    1. Look for the sign Contracted suppliers must display the “LTC Contracted” sign
    2. Ask your LTC care manager Once approved, ask your LTC care manager for the local contracted suppliers
    3. Bring your approval letter At a contracted supplier you usually pay only your own share
